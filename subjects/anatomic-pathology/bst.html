5. Neoplastic ▼
a. Benign ▼
i. Lipoma and variants ▼

  • Benign neoplasm of mature adipocytes (single large lipid droplets aka white fat).
  • Most common mesenchymal soft tissue tumor overall in adults, rarely reoccur.
  • Common on the upper back, hsoulders, neck, and abdomen.
  • 40-60 years most common.
  • Lipomas are well-circumscribed, homogenous masses typically acrally located and measuring under 5.0 cm.

a. Liposarcoma ▼

  • Tumors arising in the thigh may produce pain from compression of nerves or edema due to venous compression, while retroperitoneal LS may cause hydronephrosis or intestinal obstruction.
  • most important diagnostic feature is the presence of lipoblasts, which are pleomorphic cells containing prominent lipid vacuoles which indent the nucleus.

c. Synovial sarcoma ▼

  • Peak age being 15-30 years.
  • Sarcoma most commonly occurs in the extremities, most often in the region of the knee, foot and ankle.
  • Rarely involves the joint space despite the name (<10% of lesions).
  • Variable histologic appearance, can be biphasic (classic), monophasic fibrous, monophasic epithelial or poorly differentiated.

ii. Tenosynovial giant cell tumor ▼

  • Benign, acrally located, occuring in young/middle-aged adults. Also called 'nodular tenosynovitis.'
  • Characterized by presence of foamy macrophages (filled with clear vacuoles and often found peripherally), hemosiderin, osteoclast-like giant cells, and bland mononuclear cells.
  • Additional characteristics, often large, plump epithelial cells with brisk mitosies, but non-atypical with sclerotic/dense collagen (stromal fibrosis) throughout the tumor.
  • Can be localized-type or diffuse-type (formerly pigmented villonodular synovitis).
  • Localized-type is often a discrete lesion, commonly found on the digits, which is less aggressive and cured by excision.
  • Diffuse-type displays growth in expansive sheets with elongated villi, which can lead to local destruction. It occurs most commonly at the knee and has a high recurrence rate. Formerly known as 'Pigmented villonodular synovitis.'
  • Molecular: Blaanced translocation of 1p13 (CSF1 gene).
  • Staining: Positive for CD168 (histiocytes), CD68, CD45, and TRAP (osteoclastic giant cells)
